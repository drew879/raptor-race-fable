# Image prompts for mock-ups

Drew makes the look-and-feel mock-ups himself with an image tool (2026-11-18). These are the prompts sent to him. Each one stands on its own, so it can be pasted as is.

What's settled and baked into them: Velociraptor is the animal on the icon; the name is lettered tall and tight; amber is the warmest color on screen; hidden finds are drawn as what they are (rough pieces of amber with something trapped inside, glowing pale blue), never as circles; the journal has to look used, handwritten and full; after a level the screen shows score, amber, hidden finds, the journal page and a preview of what's next.

What's a stand-in: the home scene (Drew is exploring that himself); what is trapped inside Level 1's three hidden finds (the prompts use the three from the brief's fact list); the example numbers on the results screen.

## Icon

```text
Create a square app icon, 1024 x 1024, for a kids' dinosaur running game called Raptor Race.

Subject: Velociraptor mongoliensis, drawn as the real animal: a lean, turkey-sized feathered predator. Long, low, narrow head with a slightly dished snout that turns up a little at the tip. Slim S-curved neck. Feathers are sandy tan with rust-brown bars, a pale cream throat, and a dark stripe running back from the eye.

Pose: head, neck and the top of the shoulders only, in side view facing right, leaning forward as if sprinting flat out. Neck stretched, with the feathers on the neck and the back of the head swept back by the wind. Mouth closed. One bright amber-gold eye with a round pupil, looking ahead. The look of a hawk: fast, sharp and alert, without being scary or cute.

Style: a hand-painted natural history illustration with visible brush texture, soft warm light, and simple bold shapes that still read when the picture is the size of a fingernail. A real animal, painted with care. No cartoon eyes, no smile, no outlines, no cel shading, no photo-realism, no 3D render look.

Background: plain and dark, a deep blue-green that fades almost to black at the corners. A warm amber glow sits behind the head, brightest behind the eye, so the outline of the head is rimmed with golden light. Nothing else in the background: no plants, no ground, no sky, no eggs.

Layout: the picture fills the whole square edge to edge, with no border, no rounded corners, no frame and no drop shadow. Keep the whole head inside the middle 70% of the square. No text, no letters, no logo.

Accuracy: this is a feathered dinosaur. Feathers cover the head and neck, and only the snout is bare. No scaly, lizard-like skin on the neck. No crest, no frill, no horns. Teeth hidden behind closed lips.
```

## Home screen

```text
Design the home screen of a mobile game for kids called Raptor Race. In each level of the game you run as a different feathered raptor dinosaur in its real habitat.

Format: a tall portrait phone screen, about 9 wide by 19 tall, shown flat and filling the whole image. No phone, no hand, no device frame.

Scene: a Velociraptor in full sprint, seen from the side and slightly below, crossing the screen from left to right. It is turkey-sized and lean, with sandy tan feathers barred in rust-brown. Its wing feathers and long feathered tail stream out behind it, one foot is off the ground, and dust kicks up behind. Behind it sit a huge, low amber sun and open sky. The land is kept simple and dark so the animal's outline stands out. It should feel fast and thrilling at first glance.

Title: RAPTOR RACE across the top third, in tall, narrow, tightly spaced capital letters in cream, leaning forward a little so the name itself looks fast.

Buttons, in the bottom third: one large amber button that says PLAY. Under it, two small quiet buttons that say LEVELS and JOURNAL. A small gear in a top corner. The buttons look hand-painted, like labels cut from a notebook, with soft edges. No other text on the screen.

Style: a naturalist's expedition field journal come to life. Ink line and watercolor wash with visible brush and paper texture, painted by a skilled natural history illustrator. Warm, rich and natural, like a nature documentary filtered through a picture book. Colors: deep blue-green shadows, cream, earthy browns, and amber gold as the warmest and brightest color on screen. No neon, no cartoon style, no glossy 3D, no flat vector art, no stock app-template look.

Accuracy: the dinosaur is a real animal drawn from fossils. It is fully feathered like a bird of prey, with wings on its arms, a long stiff feathered tail and a closed mouth. No scaly movie raptor, no bare lizard skin, no roaring.
```

## Field Journal page

```text
Design one page of the Field Journal inside a mobile game for kids called Raptor Race. The journal is a naturalist's expedition notebook, and this page is about a dinosaur the player has just run as.

Format: a tall portrait phone screen, about 9 wide by 19 tall, shown flat and filling the whole image. No phone, no hand, no device frame.

The page: aged cream notebook paper that fills the screen, slightly warped, with a stitched binding down the left edge, soft stains, a mud smudge and a thumbprint. It has clearly been used outdoors. Fill the page. Sketches, notes and scraps overlap and crowd each other, with very little empty paper.

Main drawing: an ink-and-watercolor study of a Caudipteryx, a peacock-sized feathered dinosaur. It has a short, deep head with a small beak, a long neck, long legs, short arms that each carry a fan of feathers, and a fan of feathers at the tip of its tail. Its feathers are blue-black with pale bands on the fans. Loose pencil construction lines still show under the paint.

Around it: smaller pencil and ink studies of its head, one foot, a single banded tail feather, and a nest with long blue-green eggs laid in a ring. A footprint with a measurement line beside it. A tiny sketch map with an X. A pressed, fan-shaped ginkgo leaf held to the page with yellowed tape.

Handwriting: everything is written by hand in brown ink, a little uneven, with arrows pointing to parts of the drawings and one word crossed out and rewritten. An underlined heading reads "Caudipteryx", with "Jehol Forest" beneath it. Two short notes must be readable: "Its cousins sat on their nests like birds" and "Tail fan: for showing off?". Other small notes can be loose scribble. No printed type anywhere.

Hidden finds, in a row along the bottom of the page: three pieces of amber the player can find in the level, each with something trapped inside. Draw each one as a rough, lumpy nugget with its own uneven shape, never a circle and never a cut gem. Two have been found. They are painted in and glow a soft pale blue from inside. One holds a tiny feathered tail and the other holds a strand of spider web with a small insect caught in it. Each is held to the page with a strip of yellowed tape and has a handwritten label under it. The third has not been found yet. It is only a faint pencil outline of a nugget with a small question mark inside.

One small hand-painted amber arrow in the top left corner, pointing left. No other buttons.

Style: ink line and watercolor wash with visible brush and paper texture, painted by a skilled natural history illustrator in the field. Warm, rich and natural. Colors: cream paper, brown ink, earthy browns and mossy greens, amber gold as the warmest color, and pale glowing blue used only for the hidden finds. No neon, no cartoon style, no glossy 3D, no flat vector art, no clean app-template layout.

Accuracy: the dinosaur is a real animal drawn from fossils, fully feathered like a bird, with a closed mouth. No scaly movie raptor, no bare lizard skin.
```

## Results screen

```text
Design the results screen of a mobile game for kids called Raptor Race. It appears right after the player finishes a level called Jehol Forest.

Format: a tall portrait phone screen, about 9 wide by 19 tall, shown flat and filling the whole image. No phone, no hand, no device frame.

The screen is a page of an expedition notebook: aged cream paper that fills the screen, with a stitched binding down the left edge and soft stains and smudges. Everything on it is drawn, painted or written by hand. The page is full and lively, with very little empty paper.

Top: the level's name, "Jehol Forest", handwritten and underlined. Beside it, a small watercolor sketch of a newly hatched chick peeking out of a blue-green eggshell.

Score: the biggest thing on the page. "3,040" in tall, narrow, hand-painted numerals in amber gold, with the word "Score" written small above it.

Under the score, three tallies in a row. Each is a small watercolor sketch with a handwritten label:
1. A little pile of golden amber pieces, labelled "214".
2. Three pieces of amber with something trapped inside each, drawn as rough, lumpy nuggets with uneven shapes, never circles. Two are painted in and glow a soft pale blue from inside. The third is only a faint pencil outline.
3. A fallen log, labelled "1 bump".

New journal page: a small notebook page shown tilted and held on with a paper clip, with a tiny dinosaur sketch on it. "New page!" is handwritten beside it with an arrow.

What's next: along the bottom, the next level appears as a drawing the artist has only just started. It is a pencil sketch of a Velociraptor standing on the crest of a sand dune, with only the first pale washes of watercolor on it. Handwritten beside it: "Next: Gobi Desert".

Buttons at the very bottom: one large amber button that says NEXT and one small quiet button that says AGAIN. They look hand-painted, like labels cut from a notebook.

Style: ink line and watercolor wash with visible brush and paper texture, painted by a skilled natural history illustrator in the field. Warm, rich and natural. Colors: cream paper, brown ink, earthy browns and mossy greens, amber gold as the warmest and brightest color, and pale glowing blue used only for the hidden finds. No neon, no cartoon style, no glossy 3D, no flat vector art, no clean app-template layout.

Accuracy: every dinosaur is a real animal drawn from fossils, fully feathered like a bird, with a closed mouth. No scaly movie raptors, no bare lizard skin.
```

## Notes on the example numbers

The results example uses the score proposed to Drew on 2026-11-18: 10 for each piece of amber, 250 for each hidden find, and a clean-run bonus of 500 that loses 100 for each bump. So 214 amber, 2 finds and 1 bump is 2,140 + 500 + 400 = 3,040.
