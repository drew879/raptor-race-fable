Off the nest

First drop

0:16 to 0:22 · 48 m · steep

The trail tips over and the valley opens up as the music comes round again. Steep and clear, with a slalom of amber: three bells and a breath, six times.

Windfall

Jumps. A log or a creek in every bar, a boulder to keep the lanes in play, and the last three closer together.

Under the giants

Slides. Two propped trunks and the hollow log, a bar apart, then a jump and a slide close together.

The old ginkgo

0:44 to 0:58 · 112 m

The fork, which begins as the music comes round a fourth time. Left is riskier and richer: five moves three beats apart. Right is safer: two easy jumps.

Second drop

0:58 to 1:04 · 48 m · steep

The power-up, on a steep clear pitch with amber in all three lanes: the fans sweep it all in, and the bells sound as chords.

Home stretch

1:04 to 1:18 · 112 m

The last time round. Everything, mixed and three beats apart, opening with a file of young Psittacosaurus crossing the trail. Then a clear bar, and the edge.

The edge

1:18 to 1:20 · 16 m

The trail runs out over the valley, and bar 40 is the egg's dash for the edge: the whole band on the one chord that wants to go home. Swipe up to dive and catch it, and the music lands.

A bump never ends the run by itself. You only lose the egg by letting it get 26 m ahead: from the start that takes four bumps close together, late in a clean run six or more. In test runs a player who misses about a third of the obstacles still finishes, and one who misses half loses the egg. Then the picture dims and the level starts over from the nest. The level always ends at the edge. The further behind you are, the faster you gain, so a bad patch can be won back.

Where it stands

  1. Laid out to the music. 39 bars of chase, and the egg's dash for the edge is bar 40: the last bar of the music's loop, on the chord that wants to go home. The catch lands on the downbeat after it, 80 seconds in.
  2. Swipes fall on beats. Every obstacle stands 2 m past a beat, so the swipe for it lands on the beat. A jump takes off on one beat and comes down on the next. In the three teaching sections there is one move in a bar, two seconds apart. Where it gets busier they are three beats apart, a second and a half.
  3. The band plays alone first. There is no amber in bars 1 and 2. The first bell comes at bar 3, four seconds in, and the violin at bar 5 if you are running clean.
  4. Amber is the bells' part. A piece every 2 m is a bell on every eighth note, and every line starts on a beat. The five pieces over a jump are a quick run of five from take-off to landing. On the second drop the fans sweep in three lanes at once, and the bells sound as chords.
  5. The fork. Left is five moves three beats apart (jump, slide, jump, slide, jump) for 37 pieces of amber and a hidden find. Right is two logs, two bars apart, and 17 pieces. Both labels come up 32 m before the rock, 4 s ahead.
  6. A missed catch picks the run up again from the top of bar 37, six seconds before the dash, where a phrase of the music begins.
  7. 27 moves on the safe side of the fork and 30 on the risky side (28 and 31 before), and 298 pieces of amber (309 before).
  8. Still to come: the sounds redone in Live, and the home, results and Field Journal screens.
