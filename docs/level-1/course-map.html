Fork: amber on the hard side against the easy side33 to 14

A bump never ends the run by itself. You only lose the egg by letting it get 26 m ahead: from the start that takes four bumps close together, late in a clean run six or more. In test runs a player who misses about a third of the obstacles still finishes, and one who misses half loses the egg. Then the picture dims and the level starts over from the nest. The level always ends at the clearing. The further behind you are, the faster you gain, so a bad patch can be won back.

Where it stands

  1. Busier after the build 25 playtest. 28 moves on the safe side of the fork and 31 on the risky side, up from 21 and 23. Most of the long empty stretches are gone, and moves now come about a second and a half apart.
  2. The fork lives up to its promise. Left is five moves in a row (jump, slide, jump, slide, jump) for 33 pieces of amber and a hidden find. Right is two logs and 14 pieces. Both labels come up 30 m before the rock, about 3.8 s ahead, beside the trail at eye height. They can be see-through, or the game can hold still for a moment while they are read: both are in the pause sheet to try.
  3. Young Psittacosaurus cross the trail. Three of them, walking in a file across all three lanes: a jump. The dozing adult stays as scenery beside the trail.
  4. Fan out is measured in meters now. The fans stay out for 50 m, about 6.3 s at speed 8, which ends just past the last amber on the drop at any speed setting.
  5. Losing the egg ends the level. A sheet says the egg got away and Try again starts the level over. The checkpoints stay marked here in case restarts from them are wanted later.
  6. The opening. The egg stands on the rim of the nest, which sits on a shelf above a short steep pitch. Three taps from inside rock it, it hangs on the point of balance, tips over and rolls away down the slope. It plays once per visit.
  7. The ending is a catch at a cliff. The trail runs out on a spur of rock over the valley. The egg stops 9 m short of the edge and waits until the runner is right behind it. Then it rolls for the edge, and there are about two seconds to swipe up. In time, the runner dives and pins it at the very edge, turns back with it, and it hatches there. Too late, and the egg goes over: Try again picks the run up from the marked point in the home stretch, about seven seconds back.
  8. One egg size. The egg is the same size in the nest, in the chase and at the hatching. Two sizes are in the pause sheet to compare.
  9. Still to come: sound and music, and the title, results and Field Journal screens.
